Selected Papers and Accepted Work

Representative outputs connected to the AI-mediated learning and strategic cognitive offloading program. All conference papers are sole-authored.

How Debate Chatbots Based on Generative AI Transform Critical Thinking and Argumentative Writing

AECT 2025 Presented

Experimental study comparing AI debate chatbots and peer debate on critical thinking, engagement, satisfaction, and argumentative writing performance in elementary students. Employed Toulmin coding, HLM, and DGNN analysis.

Project-Based Learning with Generative AI as Cognitive Collaborators

AERA 2026 Accepted

Design-based research proposing and refining a cognitive collaborator model for higher-order thinking and creativity in project-based learning. Perfect score (5/5) in review — Division C.

The Relationship between Argumentative Discourse Sequences with Generative AI and Students' Argumentative Writing Structure

CSCL 2026 Poster Accepted

Sequential pattern mining and ENA-based analysis of learner–AI argumentative discourse and its transfer into written argumentative structure in elementary learners.

Dynamic Graph Neural Network Analysis of AI-Mediated vs. Peer Debate Discourse

Computers & Education Submitted

DGNN analysis revealing that AI-debate groups show significantly greater structural stability in argumentation transfer from debate to writing, compared to peer debate groups.

Learner–AI Collaboration and Creativity Enhancement in Elementary Education

EAIT Submitted

DDR/LAC framework study examining distributed creativity theory and strategic cognitive offloading in AI-assisted creative tasks. Grounded in Guilford's divergence-convergence framework.
